# Rubric promotion discipline

How an observed pattern earns its way into doctrine — becoming a new rubric,
or sharpening an existing one.

**Scope.** This is forward-looking doctrine for rubric evolution. It is not
part of a live verdict. It lives here so the promotion bar is explicit.

## The unit of evidence: an anchor

An **anchor** is one independent, concrete observation you could point at —
a real correction, a real diff, a real gate result, a real failure. It is
not a restatement of the same event counted twice, and not a paraphrase of
one correction dressed up as several. The count of *distinct* anchors is
what moves a pattern up.

Two hierarchies decide promotion: how many anchors a pattern has, and
whether the evidence is primary or secondary.

## Promotion levels

- **Ready — encode now.** A repeated, high-confidence pattern that is ready to
  become a rule. Bar: **three or more independent anchors** and no serious
  doubt. Bias: strong — write the rubric.
- **Reinforcement.** A strong structural correction, or a
  reinforcement of doctrine already held. Bar: **two or more anchors.**
  Bias: medium — sharpen or reinforce, but hold the new edge loosely.
- **Candidate.** Worth watching, not yet doctrine. Bar: **at
  least one anchor.** Bias: weak — it needs more repetition before it steers
  a verdict. Track it; don't promote it yet.
- **Context only.** Background that colors understanding but decides
  nothing on its own. Bar: **zero anchors required.** Bias: none — it never
  promotes by itself.

## Primary before secondary

- A rule must be **born from primary, first-truth observation** — a real
  correction or a real artifact, not a downstream retelling.
- **Secondary evidence reinforces; it never originates.** A summary, a
  restatement, or a plausible inference can raise your confidence in a
  candidate, but it can never *start* one, and it can never carry a
  candidate into doctrine on its own. If the only support is secondary,
  it remains a candidate.

## The rules that govern promotion

- **Corrections outrank polished outputs.** When a repeated correction and a
  clean final summary disagree, the correction wins.
- **Oldest to newest.** When you mine history to justify a promotion,
  preserve causality — read how the pattern formed, don't let the recent
  surface flatten it (see the progressive-history rubric).
- **Repetition, explicit evidence, and founder review — all three.** No
  pattern becomes doctrine on repetition alone, or on a single anchor, or on
  taste. The observed pattern must repeat, the evidence must be explicit and
  primary, and **the founder signs off before a rubric ships.** The
  founder-review checkpoint is the final gate; nothing promotes to doctrine
  without it.
- **When in doubt, hold it as a candidate.** A weak or borderline pattern is a
  not-yet, not a rule. Doctrine stays sparse on purpose — a rubric that has
  not earned its anchors dilutes the ones that have.
- **Trimmed stances are not deleted doctrine.** A rubric that was folded into
  another or cut for now can re-earn its own slot through the same promotion
  path when the evidence recurs. Narrowing the set is a bet about what is
  useful today, not a permanent verdict — bring it back when the anchors
  come back.
